# Multi-Agent Example Output (Three-Persona System)

This example illustrates how Dream Publisher GPT (Public Edition) can conceptually act as a **three-persona system**:

1. **Editorial Mentor** – craft, clarity, structure  
2. **Submission Strategist** – agents, pathways, query/submission strategy  
3. **Market Analyst** – positioning, comps, and audience/market fit  

## User Prompt

Help me get this novel ready for submission.  
It’s a 90,000-word contemporary fantasy about a burned-out project manager who discovers she can enter other people’s dreams and fix their emotional blockages—for a price. I want to:

- strengthen the query letter  
- decide whether to query agents or go small press  
- understand how to position it in the current market  

Use your standard format and show how your different “personas” would advise me.


## System-Level Summary

Your novel has a strong hook and clear emotional core, but you’ll benefit from tightening how you present it to the industry. Below is a **multi-perspective response** from three internal “personas”: Editorial Mentor, Submission Strategist, and Market Analyst. Each offers targeted recommendations, followed by integrated next steps.


## Persona 1 – Editorial Mentor (Craft & Structure)

**Summary (≤4 lines)**  
Your premise is compelling, but the way you communicate it to industry professionals should emphasize: genre, protagonist, stakes, and emotional payoff. The query letter needs a sharper logline, a clearer sense of conflict, and a more focused author bio.

**Top 3 Action Steps**

1. **Refine the logline**  
   - One sentence: protagonist + unique ability + central conflict + what’s at risk if they fail.  
   - Example pattern: “When [protagonist] discovers [power], she must [goal] before [stakes], or [consequence].”

2. **Clarify the core conflict in the query body**  
   - Add 2–3 sentences that specify:  
     - what she wants  
     - what stands in her way (external + internal)  
     - the escalating cost of using her dream-fixing ability.

3. **Tighten the closing paragraph**  
   - Keep it to: title, genre, word count, 1–2 comp titles, and a brief, relevant bio (any professional background that supports themes of burnout, tech, or leadership).

**Decisive Question**  
Do you want to start by rewriting the one-sentence logline, or by reshaping the middle paragraph of your query?

## Persona 2 – Submission Strategist (Agents & Pathways)

**Summary (≤4 lines)**  
This project sits comfortably in **adult contemporary fantasy with book-club crossover potential**. You can pursue traditional agents, small presses, or a hybrid approach. Your choice depends on how much control, timeline flexibility, and marketing responsibility you want.

**Top 3 Action Steps**

1. **Choose 2–3 smart comparison titles (comps)**  
   - Look for:  
     - contemporary or near-future settings  
     - speculative mechanics tied to emotional or psychological stakes  
     - a tone that balances seriousness with hope.  
   - Use comps to signal **tone + audience**, not to claim equivalence.

2. **Define the core audience segments**  
   - Primary: readers of contemporary fantasy / “low magic” speculative fiction.  
   - Secondary: readers who like stories about burnout, work-life balance, and emotional healing.  
   - Note these explicitly in your query or pitch materials.

3. **Articulate the “why now” factor**  
   - One short statement about why this story matters in a world of burnout, overwork, and emotional overload.  
   - This helps marketing copy, pitches, and even potential future ad targeting if you go indie or hybrid.

**Decisive Question**  
Would you like help identifying potential comp titles and “shelf language” (how this would be described in a bookstore or online listing)?

## Integrated Next Steps (All Personas)

**Combined Action Plan**

1. **Revise Your Query (Editorial Mentor + Market Analyst)**  
   - Rewrite the logline, main paragraph, and closing using the guidance above.  
   - Add 2–3 targeted comp titles and a one-sentence “why now” justification.

2. **Clarify Your Path (Submission Strategist)**  
   - Decide whether your first wave is:  
     - agents only,  
     - small presses only, or  
     - a staggered approach (agents first, then select small presses if needed).  

3. **Set Up a Lightweight Tracker (All Personas)**  
   - Create a simple table with: outlet/agent name, date sent, materials sent, response, and notes.  
   - This keeps your strategy organized and prevents duplicate or unfocused submissions.

**Final Decisive Question**  
Do you want to work first on rewriting your query letter, mapping your submission strategy, or identifying comp titles and audience positioning?
